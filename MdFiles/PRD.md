Act as a senior product manager and system architect. Generate a comprehensive Product Requirements Document (PRD) for the following project
Project Name : Enterprise Employee Travel & Expense
Enterprise Employee Travel & Expense Description: We are a large organization with approximately 10,000+ employees operating across multiple locations. Employees frequently travel for client meetings, training programs, audits, conferences, and internal business activities. Currently, travel requests, approvals, expense claims, and reimbursements are managed through emails, Excel sheets, phone calls, and paper documents. This process is time-consuming, lacks visibility, and creates operational challenges.
The PRD must be  well detailed, well structured and production ready. it should not follow any Generic details. it should highly emphasized according to the project requirement.
follow these Points strictly for creating the PRD:
1.Functional Requirements (Detailed) – Break down each module (Travel Requests, Expense Claims, Reimbursements, Reporting) into granular workflows, validations, and edge cases. Include step‑by‑step logic, mandatory fields, and exception handling.2.Non‑Functional Requirements (Performance, Scalability, Security) – Define response time benchmarks, concurrency limits, scalability targets, encryption standards, and compliance requirements.3.System Architecture Overview– Provide diagrams and descriptions of frontend, backend, database, integrations, and external services. Show how components interact.4.Data Model/Database Design (Tables and Relationships) – Document schema with tables like Employees, TravelRequests, ExpenseClaims, Approvals, Reimbursements. Include relationships, indexing, and normalization strategy.5.API Design (Endpoints, Request/Response Structure) – List endpoints with sample JSON payloads. Example: POST /api/travel/request, GET /api/expense/{id}, PUT /api/reimbursement/{id}/approve.6.User Flow & Journey Mapping – Map journeys for Employees, Managers, Finance, and HR/Admin. Show approval chains, claim submission, and reimbursement tracking.7.UI/UX Guidelines (Layout,Design Principles) – Define accessibility standards, responsive design, error handling, and consistent navigation principles.8.Integrations (Third‑Party Services and APIs) – Specify HRMS, Payroll, SSO, Email/Notification services. Include integration testing requirements.9.Security Considerations – Role‑based access, audit logs, encryption and fraud prevention mechanisms.10.Performance & Scalability Strategy – Caching, load balancing, horizontal scaling, database shading, and query optimization.11.Logging, Monitoring & Observability – Centralized logging, error tracking, dashboards, alerting, and audit trails.12.Testing Strategy (Unit, Integration, Edge Cases) – Define test coverage, regression testing, UAT, and automation pipelines.13.Deployment Strategy – Document CI/CD pipelines, staging vs. production environments, rollback plans, and migration strategy.14.Risks & Mitigation Plan – Technical, operational, and adoption risks with mitigation steps. Example: resistance to change mitigated by training and phased rollout.15.KPIs & Success Metrics – Processing time reduction, compliance rate, employee satisfaction, error rate, and reduction in manual intervention.16.Timeline– Define phases with realistic durations.17.Future Enhancements– AI‑driven analytics, mobile app expansion, external booking integration, fraud detection.18.Compliance & Regulatory Requirements – Explicitly document adherence to financial regulations, audit requirements, and data privacy laws.19.Audit Framework – Define immutable logs, approval tracking, and governance workflows for expense claims.20.Data Retention & Archival Policy – Define how long records are stored, archived, and retrieved for audits..
